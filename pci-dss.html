01 — When it comes up

Why companies call me about PCI DSS

PCI DSS is a contractual requirement through your acquiring bank and the card brands, not a law, but the consequences of ignoring it are real.

Your acquirer or a customer asked for proof

Merchants validate with a Self-Assessment Questionnaire or a Report on Compliance, depending on transaction volume and how they accept cards. Service providers are often asked by their customers for an Attestation of Compliance.

Your SAQ answers don’t match reality

A questionnaire chosen for the wrong integration type, or answered optimistically, is a common source of trouble after an incident.

Version 4 requirements caught you out

PCI DSS v4.0.1 is the current version, and the requirements that were future-dated became mandatory on March 31, 2025, including targeted risk analyses, broader multi-factor authentication, and controls over payment page scripts.

04 — Questions

Common questions

Straight answers to what clients usually ask first. Anything else, bring it to the call.

Are you a QSA?

No. A Qualified Security Assessor performs the formal assessment and signs a Report on Compliance. I prepare you for that assessment, or help you complete a Self-Assessment Questionnaire, which your company signs.

Which SAQ do we need?

It depends on how you accept cards: fully outsourced e-commerce, embedded payment pages, card-present terminals, or systems that store card data each point to a different questionnaire. Your acquirer has the final say.

If we use Stripe or another processor, are we out of scope?

Rarely entirely. A well-designed integration can shrink your scope to a short questionnaire, but your website and the systems that control the payment page usually remain in scope.

Is PCI DSS a law?

No. It is a standard enforced through contracts with your acquiring bank and the card brands, though some state laws reference it.
